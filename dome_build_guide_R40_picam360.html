Bio-inspired sky compass // build worksheet

Polarizing Dome + Box Mount

PiCam360 fisheye (~38×38×30 mm)  ·  dome radius R = 40 mm  ·  N = 8 gores  ·  all 8 from one film sheet, three spare
Print setting Print at 100% scale, "Actual size", fit-to-page OFF. Then measure the red 30 mm bar below each template with a ruler. If it is not exactly 30 mm, do not cut from the printout — use the hand-tracing table instead, which is scale-proof.

1 Confirm the transmission axis (do this first)

The LCD test cannot tell the pass axis from the block axis, and being 90° wrong flips the whole design. Use the glare test, which is unambiguous.

  1. Find glare bouncing off a horizontal shiny surface (an off phone screen, glass, water) at a shallow angle.
  2. Look at the glare through the film and rotate until the glare is dimmest.
  3. At that rotation the transmission axis is vertical. Draw a permanent arrow along it. This arrow, not the blue line, is your reference.
Rule Cut every gore so the transmission arrow runs lengthwise, tip to base. Assembled, the axes fan outward from the apex (radial), which makes the dark band point at the sun.

2 Gore template, 8 identical petals

Trace by hand from the table (scale-proof), or cut from the 1:1 template if the calibration bar checks out. Add an 8 mm tuck tab below the base line; fold it there later.

3 Sheet layout, one sheet is enough

Place gores with the long axis parallel to the transmission arrow (parallel to the blue line on your sheet, so vertical). Six across the 200 mm width, two rows down the 150 mm height. All eight fit on one sheet; keep the other three sheets as spares.

4 Box-lid mount with slits

Camera sits on top of the lid, lens up. Eight slits around a 40 mm octagon receive the gore tabs.

← this bar must measure 30 mm →
Lid layout at 1:1. Red = the eight slits to cut. Grey square = camera board (38 mm). Small square = cable slit.
  1. Mark the lid centre, draw the octagon (vertices 40 mm from centre), cut a slit along each of the 8 edges.
  2. Tape the camera board flat at the centre, lens up. Cut a cable slit beside it, clear of the octagon.
  3. Level the lid with a phone level, then mark North on the rim with a compass.
  4. Keep the lid from rising above the lens, or its edge will show as a dark rim at the horizon. Check with one test frame.

5 Assemble

  1. Fold each tab 90° along the base line and push it through its slit. Bases sit on the octagon, tips rise to the centre.
  2. Bring the 8 tips together at the apex, ~40 mm above the lid, aligned symmetrically.
  3. Tape the long seams from outside, the apex from inside. A tiny apex imperfection is harmless; at Turku the sun never reaches the zenith.
  4. Keep every transmission arrow tip-to-base. One rotated gore breaks the radial fan.

6 Register and capture

  • Fixed exposure and white balance, level lid, North recorded.
  • UTC timestamp on every frame; the sun position then labels each frame for free.
  • Never aim the bare lens at the sun. The compass reads the blue sky away from it.
